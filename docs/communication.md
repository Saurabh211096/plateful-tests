# Communication

Short, clear and kind. Write so the reader does not need to ask you anything back.

## Stand-up (every morning, 15 minutes)

Three lines, about 30 seconds:

1. **Yesterday:** what you finished (ticket numbers).
2. **Today:** what you will do.
3. **Blockers:** what is stopping you now, or "No blockers".

Example: "Yesterday I retested PLAT-20, it passed and I closed it. Today I write test cases for PLAT-23. No blockers."

Details and long discussions happen after the stand-up.

## EOD update (end of day, in the team channel)

```
EOD update - <your name> - <date>
Done:
- PLAT-20 retested on build 203, passed, closed
In progress:
- PLAT-23 test cases: 12 of about 20 written
Blockers:
- None
Plan for tomorrow:
- Finish PLAT-23 test cases and send them to Priya for review
```

## Asking for help

Try first (about 15 to 20 minutes), then ask with:

- what you tried,
- what you expected,
- what you got (with the error text or a screenshot).

Don't send only "Hi" and wait. Write the whole question in one message.

## Emails

- **To:** the person who must act. **CC:** people who need to know.
- Subject says what it is: "Release 1.1 - QA status - 26 Nov".
- Common short forms: **PFB** (please find below), **PFA** (please find attached), **EOD** (end of day), **MoM** (minutes of meeting), **KT** (knowledge transfer).
- Keep reply-all only when everyone needs the answer.

## Meetings

- Accept or decline invites, so the organiser knows.
- After a call with the client, send the **MoM** within the same day: decisions, action items with owner and date.
